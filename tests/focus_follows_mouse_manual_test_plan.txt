Focus Follows Mouse manual test plan

These need a real pointer, so they are not covered by ModernTests
(PaneFocusFollowsMouseTests covers the decision logic).

Setup: make run. In Settings > Pointer, turn on Focus Follows Mouse.
Use a window with two side-by-side panes, A (left) and B (right).

Moving between panes
1. Move the pointer from A to B and back. Focus follows each crossing.
2. Click in A, then press Cmd-] to move focus to B without moving
   the pointer. Wiggle the pointer inside A: focus stays in B.
   Move into B and back into A: A gets focus.
3. Move from A up onto the tab bar and back into A: nothing changes.
4. Press Cmd-] so B has focus while the pointer rests in A. Move up
   onto the tab bar and back into A: A gets focus.

Panels (find bar, composer, session note, inline AI chat, status bar
search, browser URL bar, clippings)
For 5 through 10 and 13, turn on “…even to views like Search or
Composer” under Settings > Pointer. Status bar search needs separate
status bars per pane; the shared status bar is outside every pane.
5. Open the find bar. Move the pointer onto it: the find field gets
   focus. Move onto the terminal: the terminal gets focus.
6. Repeat 5 with the composer, a session note, the inline AI chat, and
   a status bar search field.
7. With the pointer resting on A's terminal, press Cmd-F. Nudge the
   pointer around on the terminal: the find field keeps focus.
8. Open the inline AI chat with the pointer resting where the panel
   appears: the chat input keeps focus while the pointer moves around
   inside the panel. Move onto the terminal, then onto the chat's
   message list: the chat input gets focus.
9. Move the pointer onto a panel's non-text part (the find bar's
   buttons area, the composer's drag handle): the panel's text gets
   focus. Move onto A's title bar: nothing changes.
10. Focus the composer in A, move to B, then back into A over A's title
    bar: the composer gets focus, and A becomes the active pane (A
    undimmed, B dimmed).
11. Turn the checkbox off. Open the find bar in A and move between the
    find bar and A's terminal: focus doesn't change. Move to B and back
    into A: the find field gets focus again.

Windows and app switching
12. Open a second window. Moving between windows focuses the pane
    under the pointer, and the window becomes key.
13. In window 2, open the composer and leave the pointer over it. Move
    to window 1, then back into window 2 over its terminal: window 2
    gets focus, and its terminal does (the composer was moused over).
14. Cmd-Tab to another app and back without moving the pointer, with
    the pointer over a pane that doesn't have focus: focus doesn't
    change.
15. Split a pane with Cmd-D while the pointer is still: the new pane
    gets focus (Advanced setting “When focus follows mouse is enabled,
    should new split panes automatically be focused?” on, the default)
    and keeps it until the pointer moves into another pane.

Floating panes
16. tests/floating_panes/ffm_occlusion_check.sh still passes.

Browser panes
17. With a browser pane, moving the pointer into it (including from
    another window) gives the web page focus.

Stealing key focus (Advanced setting “When Focus Follows Mouse is
enabled, steal key focus even when inactive.”)
18. With another app active, move the pointer over an iTerm2 pane and
    type: the text goes to that pane and its cursor draws filled.
19. Move to another pane: typing goes there. Move off all iTerm2 panes
    onto the other app's window: typing goes to the other app again.
20. With the other app's window overlapping an iTerm2 pane, move the
    pointer over the overlap: typing goes to the other app.
